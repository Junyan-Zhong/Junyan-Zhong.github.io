The Last Five Years

On love, marriage, time, and the beauty that can remain even when a relationship ends.

The Last Five Years is probably one of the most beautiful stories about love and marriage I’ve seen in a long time. What moved me most is that it does not reduce marriage to a simple story of success or failure.

Instead, it portrays two people in their twenties meeting each other at a beautiful time in their lives, sincerely loving one another, striving for their own dreams, and giving to each other without reducing the relationship to resentment or blame.

If you see it as a love story with a time limit, it feels almost perfect. Jamie sings to Cathy when she is down. Cathy quietly watches Jamie write, her eyes full of love. Their relationship feels deeply mutual, even with all the complexity and pain that eventually comes with marriage.

There is something gentle and human about love being expressed not as a guarantee of eternity, but as the desire for a little more time together.

For the next ten minutes, we can handle that.

When I first listened to the musical while walking through New York, I thought Jamie was self-centered. After finally watching the full film, I saw their companionship differently. Their love was profound in ways outsiders can hardly understand.

Most people, including myself, tend to see the end of a marriage as proof of failure. But The Last Five Years reminded me that even relationships that end can still contain something deeply beautiful and real.

The day before, I had been reading Seamus Heaney on poetry. He resisted the idea that poetry must always justify itself through moral purpose. Art cannot lose its inventiveness, its pleasure in language, or its freedom to preserve complexity.

Between emotion and reason, I think the softness of emotion is especially precious. Art does not exist simply to judge right and wrong. Sometimes its role is to preserve the complexity of being human.

That is also why Sondheim’s musicals and Jason Robert Brown’s writing feel so powerful to me. Their characters are flawed, specific, observant, and alive. The details feel so real that the stories stop becoming “examples” of marriage and instead become portraits of people trying their best to love each other.

Maybe that is why this story stayed with me so deeply. Beneath the separation and heartbreak, it still unfolds into an enviable love story that lasted five years.

And honestly, I hope I get to experience something like that in my lifetime.

Originally written after watching the 2014 film adaptation.
