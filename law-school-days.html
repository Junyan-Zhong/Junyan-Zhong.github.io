05/26/2023

I don’t know why yesterday’s tea gathering turned into a sharing session about our families of origin.

One friend said his father dragged him around by the hair twice while he begged him to stop. The detail is that three days later, his father apologized.

Another friend said that for more than two years, he and his father lived under the same roof without speaking a single word. He had been earning money to support himself since elementary school.

Another classmate said that she opened a cabinet her mother had forbidden her to touch, took out a piece of paper, folded a crane, and showed it to her mother. Her mother threw her out of the house, and she wandered around until her father came home and she was finally allowed back in.

I thought carefully about it. My father never apologized.

Once I ran away from home and wandered around for an entire day. I thought he must have calmed down by then. When I came back, he was even practicing calligraphy. But he still chased me and beat me.

I don’t remember much else anymore.

On the day of my graduation ceremony, my younger brother and I talked on Rainbow Bridge about our own experiences. Even though we knew that other people cared about us and spent time with us, everything still somehow felt very plastic.

When we talked about our father, my brother said that for a long time he had felt as though he were living under his shadow.

“Maybe you remember him through hatred?”

My brother and I were different. Before my father died, I had already reconciled with him. My brother was different. At the time, he was still in his rebellious phase.

03/12/2023

I gradually realized that I was already in a kind of loneliness with no solution.

I used to think this feeling would come many years later, but it seems I was wrong.

To be in a relationship and still constantly feel a bone-deep loneliness leaves only endurance, with no real solution.

I think most people are probably as lonely as I am. If I do not blame the relationship, then loneliness seems to have been caused by someone else.

I have already realized that this feeling of mine may be wrong. Perhaps everyone is like this, and then there is no one to blame.

But I have to admit that often having nothing to talk about already shows that perhaps two people are not as perfectly matched as they believed.

Perhaps this desolate feeling comes from the fact that everything is preparing for the curtain to fall.

I now admit that no one can redeem this loneliness. It appears every few days and has already become my most intimate guest.

Since that is so, let me receive you properly, loneliness.

In my imagination, I pour this uninvited guest a cup of tea in a very small teacup. The tea is steaming now, but my guest looks at it and does not seem to intend to drink.

01/27/2023

The first time I read Blossoms, I found it too difficult and gave up halfway through.

The second time I read Blossoms, I could more or less accept it, but I still had little patience for the people living in the folds of the city. I kept thinking, “Isn’t this exactly the kind of life I’ve been trying so hard to escape?”

The third time, I truly got into the book.

I felt that it had included so many details of an era that had been forgotten. Jin Yucheng works in his study, and when he tells things, the details come layer upon layer. He is someone who pays attention, who sees, and who remembers.

I read it all the way from Shanghai to Shenzhen North Station. When I walked out of the station, I thought: it was only after I became able to read Blossoms that my thinking truly began to open up.

In the past, I thought freedom meant fighting for rights, “equality between men and women,” almost wishing every novel had a female protagonist. But using this kind of “correct” thinking to make demands of a writer is completely inappropriate.

Precisely because the writer values the freedom of individual writing, things can unfold in all kinds of different ways, instead of turning into the same pale, lifeless stuff.

Jin Yucheng writes about a group of people very different from himself, and he gives each of them careful detail. How open a person’s spirit must be to do that.

Now I think freedom means giving detail to the lives of people unlike yourself.

02/21/2022

Today, I read something my friend wrote:

What we call empathy is, literally, putting oneself in another’s place.

I know almost nothing about Buddhist scripture, but in the few passages I have encountered, the opening often provides a scene, somewhere for the person reciting the text to land in imagination, somewhere to sit:

One day, on a certain mountain, these people and those people gathered together. Among them, I heard the following words. Listen, and I will tell you slowly...

To be walking through a festive street and suddenly feel a chill at the back of your neck is also a kind of putting oneself in another’s place.

Empathy is allowing a ghost to pass through the body and erase my boundaries.

It is, for a moment, becoming the ghost itself.

It is walking beneath a blunt, ordinary sun and dreaming of another self.

12/14/2021

Virginia Woolf said that if a woman is going to write, she needs a room and money.

These past few days I picked up this beautiful little book again and listened to it before bed.

Now I do have a room. I have at least a little money too. But I feel as though my brain has turned to paste.

Last month I went to see Amo. For a moment I forgot the name of that poetry collection about the woman who was always drinking and always falling in and out of love.

She showed me some poems she had written too, posted in a WeChat Moments setting visible only to herself. I could not understand them. They were not like the poems she used to write, like The Hungry Girl, where I would read them and immediately feel the spirit bursting out.

I was unbearably gloomy. She is often gloomy too, holding her phone, chatting with her boyfriend, getting angry, then acting sweet with him when she sees him.

We went together for a massage by blind therapists. I was still unhappy. Then we went home and watched Lou Ye’s Blind Massage.

After the movie I went upstairs at her place to look at the moon. I was much unhappier than I had been when I was working in Hong Kong.

Every time I see Amo, I instinctively think of myself as the one who can take care of my own emotions. I still did not understand why I was so unhappy. Was it the deadline, or the lack of sex? I could not tell.

In the evening I went back to my room and made tea. The tea leaves floated on the surface of the cup.

This cup has traveled with me to many places. Chen Shu gave it to me for pour-over coffee.

One Christmas I went to Hong Kong to visit Amo. I carried a thermos filled with hot water and brought some coffee grounds. While she was selling things at a stall in Mong Kok, I made coffee beside her. This is probably the strangest memory I can think of about this cup.

To avoid the protest crowds, we walked across an empty footbridge. People scattered through the streets. Amo said she had made no money that Christmas. I had no sense of direction and simply followed her. Inside I felt calm, except for worrying that a stray bullet might pass across the bridge.

Looking at the tea leaves slowly unfurling in the brown cup, I was not thinking much. I was only thinking about Chen Shu liking all of my recent Douban posts, imagining her going through them one by one and clicking “like.” It felt somehow comforting.

I blew the tea leaves toward one side of the cup. When I tried to drink, they drifted lazily back again. After blowing them away a few times, I suddenly felt like crying.

I made up my mind not to look at anything related to tomorrow’s class.

I picked up The Distant Sunflower Fields and started again from the beginning, and it felt almost as though I had never read it before.

For a while I often wondered what gift I could give Professor Sun, whom I admired very much. Every day I would look at my bookshelf and think about which book was presentable enough. This one seemed right. It could show my taste. I imagined him reading it while waiting at the airport.

I had once scrolled far back through his old WeChat posts and remembered that he liked Yu Xiuhua’s poetry. I thought perhaps he would like Li Juan too.

But in the end I never gave him the book.

One day I opened a bag of pistachios. Professor Sun was smoking, so while waiting for him to finish I had to start eating them myself.

When he finished smoking, I offered him some. Judging from my stiff posture, I clearly had little experience offering food to other people. He reluctantly took a few.

At that moment I realized sadly that even inviting someone to eat something, or giving someone a gift, requires talent. And I, having grown up poor, had obviously lacked the chance to practice.

I read to page 112, then fell asleep on the bed with a hot-water bottle, my head heavy against my hand.

When the light woke me, my cheek and ear were aching.

I told Amo: I am living a boring life full of forgetting.

I got up at six in the morning, put on blond Eckbert in German, and then drifted back to sleep.

I dreamed that several classmates and I were hiking toward Tibet. The mountains on both sides of the national highway seemed as though they might press down on us. There was almost no vegetation.

It was getting dark, so I thought I had to find somewhere to stay. I stopped at a random house and asked whether I could spend the night. Then it started to rain. I could hear the rain tapping all around the room, and water was splashing in through the gaps.

I opened a glass door and discovered that my classmates were staying in a hotel just across a patch of grass. One of them was MH. He ran over, saying, “You’re here!” I immediately started worrying about how to tell the kind person who had taken me in that I wanted to go stay with my classmates. MH slipped and got his clothes wet.

Later I dreamed that several of us were sleeping side by side, like family. Both sides of my arms felt warm. A classmate beside me was reading a diary aloud. There were simple black line drawings on the page.

I listened carefully and thought: so this classmate is speaking German, and speaking it so well. The pronunciation was so standard. Suddenly German felt like such a warm language.

Then I woke up. It was my phone reading German aloud. (Laughs.) The blanket really was warm, though. Listening to a classmate read German beside me felt very comforting.
